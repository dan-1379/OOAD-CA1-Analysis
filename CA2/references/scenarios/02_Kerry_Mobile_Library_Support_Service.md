# **CA1 Scenario 02: Kerry Mobile Library Support Service** 

### **Scenario CA1-02-KMLS  |  Kerry Rural Library Partnership** 

**How to use this scenario** This document provides the case evidence for your assigned CA1 analysis. Complete the work and submission described in the main CA1 specification. Where the evidence is incomplete or inconsistent, identify the uncertainty rather than inventing a confirmed policy. 

## **Organisation and current service** 

Kerry Rural Library Partnership operates a mobile library service for villages and rural communities that do not have convenient access to a permanent library branch. Two vehicles follow recurring routes and stop at community centres, schools, sheltered-housing complexes and agreed roadside locations. Each stop has a usual day, arrival time and visit duration. 

Borrowers use the mobile service to return items, borrow available material and collect books or other resources requested in advance. Some borrowers also use permanent branches, so account and loan information is shared with the wider library service. The mobile team wants better support for route planning, requested materials, borrower communication and service disruption. 

## **Routes, stops and schedules** 

Routes are planned several months in advance using a spreadsheet. A route contains an ordered set of stops, but the sequence may change because of road works, school closures, local events or vehicle access. Some locations are visited weekly and others fortnightly or monthly. A stop may be temporarily suspended without removing it from the normal route. 

Staff record planned arrival times, but travel conditions make exact times difficult to guarantee. A vehicle may reach a stop early, late or not at all. Drivers sometimes shorten a visit to recover time, although community coordinators believe a minimum visit period should be protected. Staff have not agreed how much delay should trigger a notification. 

A replacement vehicle has less shelving and accessibility equipment than the main vehicles. When a vehicle changes, staff may need to reduce the material carried or move a service to a nearby building. Changes are currently shared through telephone calls, email, community noticeboards and social media, but the messages are not always consistent. 

## **Borrowers and requested materials** 

A borrower may request a specific title, a particular edition or any suitable item on a topic. Requests may be placed through a branch, by telephone or during a mobile-library visit. Staff record some requests in the library system and others on a printed route sheet for later entry. 

A requested item may be available at a branch, already on loan, in transit or reserved for another borrower. Staff sometimes bring an alternative title if the exact item is unavailable. A borrower may accept the alternative, keep the original request active or cancel the request. This choice is not always recorded. 

Items intended for a mobile stop must be selected, assigned to the correct route and loaded before departure. Similar place names and handwritten notes have led to material being loaded onto the wrong vehicle. Space is limited, particularly when schools request class sets or several borrowers have large orders. 

## **Visit preparation and service delivery** 

Before a route begins, staff review expected collections, outstanding requests, returns awaiting transport to branches and any location-specific information. Requested materials should be checked against the route manifest, but time pressure sometimes means staff rely on labelled bags or memory. 

At a stop, a member of staff records loans and returns using a connected device. If connectivity is unavailable, transactions are written on a temporary sheet and entered later. An item may therefore appear available elsewhere even though it has already been issued. Duplicate or incomplete entries have occurred when temporary records were entered by different staff. 

A community coordinator may collect requested material for several registered borrowers. Staff are uncertain what evidence of permission is sufficient, especially where the borrower has not used this arrangement before. Schools may also nominate different staff members to collect class materials. 

## **Disruptions, missed collections and follow-up** 

Weather, vehicle faults, road closures and staff availability can cause a route or individual stop to be delayed, relocated or cancelled. Staff try to distinguish between a delayed visit and a cancelled visit, but the final status may not be recorded until the vehicle returns to the depot. 

When a visit is cancelled, requested items may remain on the vehicle, return to a branch or be transferred to the next route. Borrowers may prefer to wait, collect from another location or cancel the request. Staff need to know which option was agreed so that material is not released incorrectly. 

Borrowers occasionally miss collection at the expected stop. Staff currently decide how long to retain the item based on demand, the next planned visit and available vehicle space. There is no single retention period for all materials. 

## **Information and organisational context** 

The route coordinator needs reliable information about future routes, changed stops, materials awaiting loading, uncollected requests, incomplete offline transactions and recurring disruptions. Community partners want sufficient notice of changes, while staff do not want to publish borrower information. 

The service has a small permanent team and uses relief drivers and temporary staff. A driver may need route and stop information without access to full borrower records. Mobile connectivity is unreliable in some areas. The initial scope is the operation of the two mobile-library vehicles and their community stops; replacement of the wider librarymanagement system is not proposed. 

## **Stakeholder evidence** 

- Route coordinator: “A route is not just a list of places. The order matters, but it may change on the morning of the visit.” 

- Mobile-library staff member: “If we work offline, another branch may still think an item is on the shelf.” 

- Community coordinator: “People need to know whether the van is late or whether it is not coming at all.” 

- Borrower: “If my requested book is not available, I might take a similar one, but I do not always want the original request cancelled.” 

- Driver: “The replacement vehicle cannot carry everything that fits in the main vehicle.” 

- Branch staff member: “A request can be recorded in several places, and we do not always know which note is current.” 

## **Unresolved matters** 

The scenario does not confirm every policy or decision. Clarification may be required about: 

- how much delay should trigger a notification or service-status change; 

- whether a community coordinator may collect for another borrower and what permission is required; 

- how requested material should be prioritised when vehicle capacity is limited; 

- how long an uncollected item should remain assigned to a route; 

- when an alternative item should replace or coexist with the original request; 

- how offline transactions should be reconciled and checked for duplicates; 

- what information relief drivers and community coordinators should be permitted to see.
