# **CA1 Scenario 03: Volunteer Transport Coordination** 

# **Service** 

### **Scenario CA1-03-VTCS  |  Kerry Community Link** 

**How to use this scenario** This document provides the case evidence for your assigned CA1 analysis. Complete the work and submission described in the main CA1 specification. Where the evidence is incomplete or inconsistent, identify the uncertainty rather than inventing a confirmed policy. 

## **Organisation and current service** 

Kerry Community Link coordinates non-emergency journeys for residents who have difficulty using ordinary public transport. Journeys include community activities, shopping, education, social appointments and essential local services. Volunteer drivers use their own cars, while partner organisations occasionally provide accessible vehicles. Requests, driver availability and completed journeys are recorded through telephone notes, email, spreadsheets and printed weekly schedules. Different coordinators may describe the same destination or passenger need differently, and changes are not always reflected on every copy. 

## **Journey requests and passenger needs** 

A passenger may request a one-way journey, return journey or recurring series. A request normally includes collection and destination locations, required arrival time, expected return time, contact details and any practical assistance needs. Requests are sometimes submitted by relatives or community workers without confirming who should receive updates. 

Passengers may need wheelchair space, room for a walking aid, assistance reaching the vehicle or permission for a companion. The service records practical transport information rather than detailed health information. Staff are unsure how to handle sensitive details included unnecessarily by a referrer. 

Some services are intended for registered members while occasional community journeys may be open more widely. Membership status, contribution arrangements and priority for essential journeys are not applied consistently. 

## **Volunteer drivers and availability** 

Volunteer drivers provide usual availability, preferred areas, maximum journey distance and any vehicle limitations. Availability may change weekly. A volunteer can decline a journey without giving a reason, but repeated unsuccessful offers make planning difficult. 

Drivers have completed different checks and training. Some may carry passengers who need light assistance, while accessible vehicles require specifically authorised drivers. Staff occasionally allocate a familiar driver before checking whether required documentation remains current. 

A driver may be offered several compatible journeys. Coordinators try to avoid overlapping commitments and excessive waiting, but provisional offers are recorded inconsistently. 

## **Matching and journey planning** 

Coordinators consider passenger needs, driver availability, vehicle suitability, locations and times. Passengers travelling to nearby destinations may share a journey if capacity, timings and preferences permit. Staff disagree about whether sharing should be assumed or explicitly agreed each time. 

Recurring journeys may be allocated to the same driver for continuity, but this can create dependence on one volunteer. When no driver is available, staff may place the request on a waiting list, seek help from a partner or advise the passenger that transport cannot be provided. 

Drivers receive a schedule containing necessary collection, contact and assistance information. Printed schedules may become outdated after changes, and drivers do not all use email or mobile applications. 

## **Changes, completion and follow-up** 

Passengers may cancel, alter a time or change the destination. Drivers may become unavailable because of illness, vehicle problems or weather. A replacement must meet the original passenger and vehicle requirements. 

The driver records collection, arrival, return and any unsuccessful journey. A passenger may not be ready or may not appear. Drivers currently decide how long to wait and whom to telephone. Reasons for incomplete journeys are not recorded consistently. 

The coordinator needs information about unallocated requests, provisional offers, confirmed journeys, driver workload, recurring unmet demand and incomplete records. Volunteers should not see financial notes or unrelated passenger history. The initial scope excludes emergency transport and vehicle tracking. 

## **Stakeholder evidence** 

- Coordinator: “Finding any driver is not enough; the driver and vehicle must suit the passenger and the journey.” 

- Volunteer driver: “A printed schedule is useful until one collection time changes.” 

- Passenger: “I need to know whether the return journey is confirmed, not only how I am getting there.” 

- Community worker: “I may submit the request, but the passenger should still receive the final arrangements.” 

- Accessible-vehicle coordinator: “The vehicle can be free while no authorised driver is available.” 

- Volunteer coordinator: “We cannot tell whether a driver declined, was unavailable or was never contacted.” 

## **Unresolved matters** 

The scenario does not confirm every policy or decision. Clarification may be required about: 

- eligibility and priority when demand exceeds available drivers; 

- whether consent to journey sharing is general or required for each journey; 

- when a provisional offer reserves a driver; 

- how recurring journeys should be reviewed and reassigned; 

- the maximum waiting time for a passenger who is not ready; 

- which information may be shared with drivers and partner organisations; 

- how unsuccessful offers, cancellations and unmet demand should be categorised.
