# **CA1 Scenario 05: Rural Health Appointment Transport** 

# **Service** 

### **Scenario CA1-05-RHATS  |  South Kerry Community Travel Network** 

**How to use this scenario** This document provides the case evidence for your assigned CA1 analysis. Complete the work and submission described in the main CA1 specification. Where the evidence is incomplete or inconsistent, identify the uncertainty rather than inventing a confirmed policy. 

## **Organisation and current service** 

South Kerry Community Travel Network is a not-for-profit service that helps eligible residents travel from rural communities to planned healthcare appointments. The network does not provide emergency transport. Journeys are completed by trained volunteer drivers using their own cars and by two accessible vehicles operated by employed drivers. 

Requests arrive by telephone, referral email or through local support organisations. Staff use a shared calendar, spreadsheet and paper driver sheets to coordinate journeys. Demand has increased, and staff want more reliable support for eligibility, passenger needs, driver assignment, changes and confidential information. 

## **Transport requests and eligibility** 

A request normally includes the passenger, appointment location, appointment time, estimated duration, collection address and any travel needs. Referrers sometimes provide an appointment date without the expected finishing time, or use a family member as the contact. Staff may need several calls before a request is ready for allocation. 

The service is intended for people who have no reasonable transport alternative and who can travel safely using the available vehicles. Regular passengers are familiar to staff, but new passengers may require an eligibility discussion. Staff are uncertain how frequently eligibility should be reviewed when circumstances change. 

The service records practical travel needs such as wheelchair space, assistance entering a vehicle, room for a walking aid, the presence of a companion and whether a passenger can share a journey. Staff do not require clinical diagnoses, but referrers occasionally include them in emails or notes. 

## **Planning and driver allocation** 

Staff try to allocate a suitable driver and vehicle while limiting unnecessary travel. A driver may complete one journey or carry compatible passengers travelling to nearby locations. Shared journeys can be efficient, but appointment times, passenger needs, vehicle capacity and the acceptability of sharing must be considered. 

Volunteer drivers provide general availability and may decline individual journeys. Some volunteers prefer familiar areas or do not drive after dark. Accessible vehicles require authorised drivers and may already be committed to another passenger. Staff sometimes telephone several drivers before securing an allocation, but unsuccessful contacts are not recorded consistently. 

A journey may be provisionally assigned while staff wait for the passenger, referrer or driver to confirm. Staff disagree about whether a provisional assignment should prevent the driver from being offered another journey. 

## **Journey confirmation and completion** 

Passengers should receive confirmation of the collection window, driver or service contact, expected arrangements and any contribution towards travel costs. Exact collection times can be difficult to promise because previous appointments may overrun. Drivers need directions and practical assistance information, but should not receive unrelated personal details. 

The driver records collection, arrival and return completion. A passenger may not be ready, may not be at the agreed address or may decide not to travel. A driver may wait, contact the coordinator or leave, depending on circumstances. There is no agreed maximum waiting time for every journey. 

Some appointments have uncertain finishing times. A driver may wait nearby, return later or a different driver may complete the return journey. Staff do not always record the outward and return arrangements separately, which can cause confusion when plans change. 

## **Changes, cancellations and disruption** 

Appointment times may change at short notice. Passengers may cancel, request a companion, need a different collection address or be kept longer than expected. Drivers may become unavailable because of illness, vehicle problems or severe weather. 

Staff try to find a replacement without disclosing passenger information more widely than necessary. If no suitable transport can be provided, the passenger and referrer should be informed promptly. It is unclear who should be contacted first when the passenger cannot be reached. 

Repeated late cancellations reduce driver availability and may create unnecessary travel, but staff do not want to disadvantage passengers whose appointments are changed by a healthcare provider. Cancellation reasons are recorded inconsistently. 

## **Information and organisational context** 

The coordinator needs reliable views of unallocated requests, provisional journeys, confirmed journeys, accessiblevehicle demand, driver availability, changes and incomplete journey records. Summary reports are required for funders, but should not identify passengers unnecessarily. 

Office staff, volunteer drivers, employed drivers and referring organisations need different information. Mobile coverage is uneven and some volunteers prefer printed journey sheets. The first release should coordinate planned transport; emergency dispatch, direct access to healthcare records and clinical decision-making are outside scope. 

## **Stakeholder evidence** 

- Coordinator: “The appointment time is fixed, but the finishing time may be little more than a guess.” 

- Volunteer driver: “I need enough information to help the passenger safely, but I do not need a medical history.” 

- Passenger: “Please tell me whether the driver is waiting or coming back later. That is often the part I am unsure about.” 

- Referrer: “A request can change after I send it, especially when the clinic changes the appointment.” 

- Accessible-vehicle driver: “Wheelchair space and companion seating must be known before the vehicle is assigned.” 

- Office staff member: “We sometimes call several volunteers, but the spreadsheet only shows the person who finally accepted.” 

## **Unresolved matters** 

The scenario does not confirm every policy or decision. Clarification may be required about: 

- the evidence and review period for passenger eligibility; 

- which practical needs may be shared with a driver and which information should remain restricted; 

- whether a provisional assignment reserves a driver or vehicle; 

- how outward and return stages should be represented when different drivers are involved; 

- the maximum waiting time when a passenger is not ready or an appointment overruns; 

- how cancellation responsibility should be recorded when a provider changes the appointment; 

- who should be notified first when arrangements fail and the passenger cannot be reached.
